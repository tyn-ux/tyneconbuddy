When does opportunity cost change?

Textbook p.15–21, in simple English. Read, try the slider, then do the 8-question quiz.

Opportunity cost = the value of the best option you give up.

So there is only one question to ask: has what I give up changed?

You give up two kinds of things: what you pay (money, time, risk), and the best option you cannot have.

On 4 February 2024, Inter Miami played a Hong Kong team at Hong Kong Stadium. About 40,000 tickets sold out fast. Most fans came to see Lionel Messi. He had a leg injury and sat on the bench for all 90 minutes. Fans shouted "refund".

The five situations

Each one is short. Read the answer, then read the example.

1. The chosen option changes in value

Cost: no change. Only the benefit changes.

Messi does not play. Watching the match is worth less. But the fan still gives up the same ticket money and the same evening. Cost is the same.

2. You pay more money or spend more time on it

Cost: moves the same way. Pay more → cost up. Pay less → cost down.

In April 1966, the Star Ferry first-class fare went up from 20 cents to 25 cents. The boat and the trip were the same, but crossing now cost 5 cents more. (The rise also led to protests and riots in Kowloon, though historians say people were angry about poverty and corruption too, not just the fare.)

3. The best option you gave up changes in value

Cost: moves the same way.

In 1849, gold was found in California. Sailors arriving in San Francisco left their ships to look for gold, and hundreds of empty ships sat in the bay. Their pay had not fallen. Staying on the ship just became very costly, because what they gave up became much better.

4. Another option (not the best one) changes in value

Cost: it depends. Falls → no change. Rises → cost goes up only if it passes the best option you gave up.

A student chooses to study. Second choice: hiking. Third choice: a concert. If the concert becomes better but is still below hiking, nothing changes. If it passes hiking, it becomes the new best option given up, so the cost goes up.

5. The risk of something bad goes up

Cost: moves the same way. You may give up money, health or safety.

In July 2013, heavy rain and five matches in a few days turned the Hong Kong Stadium pitch to mud. A Tottenham player was carried off injured. Manchester United still played there on 29 July, but the risk of injury was much higher, so playing cost them more.

Exam wording to keep
Opportunity cost is the value of the highest-valued option forgone. A change in the value of the chosen option does not affect it, because the value of the highest-valued option forgone is not affected.
Two traps.
1. Money already spent that you cannot get back is a historical cost. It changes nothing. (p.23)
2. If a question asks "will the cost necessarily remain unchanged?" and the cost may rise, the answer is No.
